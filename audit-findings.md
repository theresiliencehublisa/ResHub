# Website and App Clarity Audit — Working Findings

## Website findings

The website predominantly uses Australian spelling: **judgement**. Its app language currently calls the product a “support app” and “companion,” but it does not yet consistently label it as the **free, self-guided AI app** or contrast it beside the **real personal 8-week program with Juan**. The program content is strong but should be labelled “real personal 8-week program with Juan” at key decision points, not only in the FAQ.

## Linked app findings

The app login page currently uses the US spelling **judgment-free**. It describes “AI guides,” “an optional 8-week plan,” “a private journal,” and “self-help toolkit,” but needs a clear persistent label that it is the **free, self-guided AI app** and is not the real personal program.

The app’s onboarding privacy text states that chat and journal writing are sent to an AI service to allow replies; signed-in guides may remember past conversations and retain a few plan notes. This conflicts with the requested blanket claim, “Everything you type stays private. It is not shared with anyone.” The website and app must therefore not make that absolute claim unless the app’s underlying data behaviour changes and is independently confirmed.

The app already references 000 and Lifeline 13 11 14, but the user-requested crisis wording should be made more prominent and applied consistently.

The next app welcome screen describes Rex as a safe place to talk and introduces Juan and Carlos Camacho, then uses the button label “Ready for the full journey.” This can reasonably be mistaken for direct access to the real personal program. A clearer label would identify it as the **free self-guided AI app** and say that the real personal 8-week program is arranged directly with Juan.

The 27-screen app tour ends with “I’m ready.” The app’s tour language should consistently say “self-guided app journey,” and the final action should say “Start the free self-guided app.” A separate persistent action should offer “Ask about the real personal program with Juan.”

The app then asks whether “Carlos” should “put together a personalised 8-week plan,” with a button reading “Yes, build my 8-week plan.” This is the most direct point of confusion because it presents an in-app plan as personalised and attached to a named professional. It should be changed to “Start my self-guided 8-week app plan” and followed by a separate choice, “Ask Juan about the real personal 8-week program.”

During the anonymous preview walkthrough, the guides-and-toolkit path begins a four-step set-up flow. This is an appropriate point to repeat, in short form, that the user is entering the free self-guided app—not enrolling in the real personal program.

The self-guided set-up asks users about recent wellbeing and sensitive areas including housing, health, alcohol or other substance use, legal or justice matters, grief, and feeling alone. Because these responses can be sensitive, the app should display a concise accurate privacy explanation before this step and keep the “Need help now?” crisis action highly visible. The app’s current safety wording should be replaced consistently with: “This is a support tool — NOT a crisis or emergency service. If you are in danger, call 000 or Lifeline 13 11 14 immediately.”

The fourth set-up screen asks directly, “How safe do you feel in yourself right now?” and includes “I’ve had thoughts of hurting myself.” The “Need help now?” control remains visible, but the exact 000 and Lifeline instruction should also appear directly above the choices, in high-contrast text, rather than only behind a separate action.

The app’s **Resilience & Recovery Program** page is the strongest existing explanation of the real personal program: it describes a free, individually tailored 8-week program built with Juan, a support team, intake forms, and an appointment process. It should be labelled consistently as the **Real personal 8-week program with Juan** to distinguish it from the self-guided app plan. It uses the US spelling “judgment” and says Juan is available “24 hours a day, 7 days a week” and “any time.” Those contact claims should sit beside the exact non-emergency message so users do not mistake the program for a crisis service.

The real program’s Privacy & Confidentiality Notice explicitly covers collection of personal, health, legal/program, and communication information. It says information may be shared with listed program and medical professionals with written consent, or in limited safety and legal situations. Therefore, the real program must never be described using the app-only phrase “Everything you type stays private. It is not shared with anyone.” The website must distinguish the app privacy explanation from the program’s separate consent-based privacy process.

The app landing page still says “judgment-free,” repeats the generic “optional 8-week plan,” and describes the app only as a support tool. It needs a prominent first-line label: **“Free self-guided AI support app — not the real personal 8-week program with Juan.”**
